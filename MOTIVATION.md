# Motivation

## Situation

* Mission-driven organizations (NGOs, research collectives, small initiatives) increasingly rely on **social media to communicate their work, build audiences, and mobilize support**.
* These organizations often operate with **limited staff capacity, limited budgets, and volunteer-driven workflows**.
* Social media activity typically accumulates over time across multiple platforms (e.g., YouTube, Substack, X, Facebook, Bluesky, websites).
* Responsibility for social media often **changes hands between staff, volunteers, or collaborators**, leading to fragmented institutional memory.

Relevant research shows that nonprofits rely heavily on social media but often lack the resources and analytic capacity to manage it strategically (Lovejoy & Saxton, 2012, *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*).

## Complication

* Conducting a proper **audit of social media history and performance** currently requires:

  * multiple analytics tools
  * manual data collection
  * platform-specific analysis
* This process is **time-consuming, expensive, and fragmented**.
* Small organizations therefore often **do not perform systematic analysis**, even though it is crucial for effective strategy.
* Consequences include:

  * missed insights about what content works
  * weak engagement strategies
  * inconsistent messaging across time
  * difficulty onboarding new team members who inherit a disorganized social media presence
* Even when analysis is attempted, it often:

  * compares nonprofits with **corporate accounts**, producing misleading benchmarks
  * fails to account for **budget and staffing differences**

## Question

* Can an **AI-assisted system automatically audit and analyze an organization’s social media presence**, producing strategic insights at **low cost and high speed**?
* More specifically:

  * Can such a system aggregate historical data across platforms?
  * Can it generate clear metrics, visualizations, and strategic interpretation?
  * Can it benchmark performance against **comparable mission-driven organizations**?

## Hypothesis

The proposed tool described above could provide a solution by:

* automatically aggregating social media history across platforms
* producing **engagement timelines, metrics, and thematic analysis**
* generating a **clear snapshot of current communication performance**
* benchmarking against **similar organizations rather than corporate actors**

In doing so, the system would enable **data-informed social media strategy for small organizations** without requiring large budgets, specialized staff, or lengthy manual analysis.
